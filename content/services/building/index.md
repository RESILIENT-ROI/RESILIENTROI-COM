For architecture practices developing a method based on ours. Only the parts of the DBJ Method they need, one chapter at a time. We encapsulated TOGAF so that any role distant from architecture sees benefits, not confusion.

## A method you adopt and adapt

The [DBJ Guide](https://guide.dbj.org/) is the companion to the [DBJ Method](https://method.dbj.org/). It describes a set of architecture practices that an organisation can take on and shape for its own use: its own structure, its own terminology and its own processes.

## The chapters

Each chapter covers one part of the method, so a practice can start with the parts it needs.

1. **[Taxonomy](https://guide.dbj.org/taxonomy/).** A two-level hierarchy of four categories, each with four capabilities. It is the single source of truth for structure and terminology.
2. **[Capability Maturity Model](https://guide.dbj.org/cmm/).** Raising maturity across the whole organisation, not only the architecture practice.
3. **[Projects steering](https://guide.dbj.org/adm/).** A simplified Architecture Development Method with five steps, each tied to a level of the taxonomy, producing the architecture deliverables.
4. **[BPT](https://guide.dbj.org/bpt/).** Three decoupled domains, Business, Product and Technology, running in parallel and communicating both ways.
5. **[AI primer](https://guide.dbj.org/ai/dbj-ai-primer/).** AI as an overarching concern, with the model, the LLM and the harness treated as separate abstractions.
6. **[Technical debt and legacy knowledge](https://guide.dbj.org/technical_debt/distributed_legacy_strategy/).** Knowledge and technical debt treated as separate issues.

## Architecture for roles outside architecture

The simplified development method keeps the architecture discipline while making it usable by business, product and technology people. Because the maturity model is aimed at the whole organisation, architects and the wider teams they work with share one vocabulary and one way of steering projects.
