An organisation's key performance indicator is the sum of the capabilities and maturities of its people. That is the key for adopters to enter the new operating model and mitigate the risks of the AI storm. They learn to decide where their organisation stands today, and what it takes to reach the level where work at AI speed can safely start. The ROI follows.

## AI amplifies what is already there

AI does not create order. It speeds up whatever order, or disorder, already exists in your processes. Applied to chaotic processes, it produces chaotic results, only faster. Without defined processes, results are unreliable and oversight is weak. That is why maturity comes first.

## The DBJ Capability Maturity Model

The [DBJ CMM](https://method.dbj.org/cmm/index.html) is a version of TOGAF's Capability Maturity Model tailored for business. It is meant for every role except architects, who already work with the full model.

- Maturity is scored from **L0 to L5**, mapped one-to-one onto TOGAF's M0 to M5.
- **L3** is the required starting point for contributing; **L5, Optimising,** is the target.
- Each person passes the DBJ CMM exam before doing any work inside the Business ↔ Products ↔ Technology loop.

## Measuring the organisation

The DBJ CMM level is the organisation's essential KPI. It is measured after training, through a maturity assessment, and it measures what the organisation is now able to deliver rather than what it has delivered in the past. Where the operating model is set up for a single product, the maturity of those product teams is measured instead.

## When AI joins the work

Below L5, the organisation improves its operations by conventional means, without AI. At L5, AI becomes part of the Business ↔ Products ↔ Technology cycle: it speeds up translation between domains, surfaces risks that span several areas, and helps refine the architecture practice. From there it becomes an engine for continuous improvement.
