An organisation's key performance indicator is the sum of its people's capabilities and maturity. That is the key for adopters to enter the new operating model and mitigate the risks of the AI storm. They learn to decide where their organisation stands today, and what it takes to reach the level where work at AI speed can safely start. The ROI follows.

## Measuring maturity

Onboarding uses the DBJ Capability Maturity Model (DBJ CMM). It scores an organisation on five elements that exist in every organisation, whatever its size or industry:

- **Governance:** who decides, the policies, and whether they are followed.
- **Skilled resource pool:** the people and the skills the organisation has.
- **Projects and portfolios:** how work is started, run and delivered.
- **Business operations:** the running business, day to day.
- **Architecture repository:** where decisions, patterns and records are kept.

Each element is scored from L0 (none) to L5 (optimising), the same scale as TOGAF's M0 to M5. The element assessment is the lowest of the five scores: a chain is as strong as its weakest link.

## How onboarding runs

Every engagement begins with the DBJ CMM Training. Typically a cross-functional team, representing all roles, learns the maturity model and the common taxonomy before any architecture work begins. The method requires each person to pass the DBJ CMM L5 exam before working inside the Business, Products, Technology loop.

The Maturity Assessment is taken after the training. It measures the organisation not by what it has delivered, but by what it is now able to deliver.

The method requires organisational maturity at L5 before work inside the loop starts. Where a customer asks, an engagement can begin at L3 instead. That is a negotiated exception, not the method: it is recorded, with the reasons, in a Business Decision Record; more exceptions are to be expected; there is no AI acceleration until L5 is reached; and the engagement includes the climb to L5.
