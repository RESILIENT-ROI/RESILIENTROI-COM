Every mature organisation reaches the point where the business value it encapsulates is worth more than the software it has built or uses. Your old systems hold years of business knowledge. We enable your teams to take it out and keep it safe before anything is replaced, so modernisation does not throw it away with the old code.

## Technical debt is business value held in legacy technology

Refactoring old code on its own is a wide, slow circle that leads back to where it started. Preservation starts from the business knowledge instead: what the old system does for the business, written down and kept in a store the Products domain sets up, separate from the code.

## How the knowledge is taken out

1. Business starts an initiative to remove a piece of legacy.
2. Business and Products declare the legacy processes and the technical debt involved.
3. Products sets up a store for the knowledge.
4. Business decides the new products and selects the first one.
5. Business and Products declare the new project.
6. Business analysts decouple the knowledge from the legacy processes; Products and Technology decouple it from the legacy components. Steps 5 and 6 repeat while there is more legacy to decouple.
7. Business and business analysts decide the conceptual and logical architecture; business analysts and engineers decide the physical architecture; Technology decides the application architecture. Then engineers start the project.

## Starting at the top

The DBJ Method analyses the existing IT landscape across four levels of abstraction: conceptual, logical, physical and implementation. Design problems and technical debt show up at the higher levels first, and there is no need to dive into the code if the design is wrong.
