
# /buyer-fit Report

Fetched the live homepage and team page, plus the local terms/privacy/team content for the trust-signal and entity-consistency checks. Findings below, ranked by buyer impact (highest first).

## 1. No evidence anywhere on the page
- **Gap:** No case study, client name, outcome, or number anywhere on the site.
- **Why it matters:** This buyer is primed to distrust anything that sounds like a "magic ingredient" pitch. Without one verifiable data point, every claim on the page — maturity, operating model, ROI — is pure assertion, which is exactly the pattern this buyer has learned to discount.
- **Proposed Fix:** Add one concrete proof point near "What We Do" — even an anonymised outcome ("took one client's dev org from DBJ CMM L2 to L4 in under two months") turns the page from assertion into evidence.

## 2. Enablement item reads as AI-speed/output hype
- **Claim:** "The result is a steady stream of AI-enabled products or services, all resilient, safe and compliant."
- **Why it matters:** This is the first service item after the hero. It's precisely the kind of tools/speed/AI-hype promise this buyer rejects outright — they already suspect their problem is structural, not an output-speed problem, so leading with an output promise loses them immediately.
- **Proposed Fix:** Lead with the diagnosis, not the output: "Most AI delivery problems aren't in the code — they're in how business, product and technology teams work together. We raise your organisation's maturity so that gap closes."

## 3. "Building" item targets a different buyer, mid-pitch
- **Claim:** "For architecture practices developing a method based on ours."
- **Why it matters:** This is one of four equally-weighted "What We Do" items, but it's written for other architecture practices, not the Traditional-org SME buyer reading the rest of the page. It plants a "wait, who is this actually for?" doubt right in the core pitch.
- **Proposed Fix:** Move it off the core four-item list to a separate partners/licensing page (linked from the footer), or add a one-line audience qualifier so the SME reader can tell it's not meant for them and move on confidently.

## 4. Hero tagline doesn't name the buyer's pain
- **Claim:** "Work is measured by what it returns. For years to come."
- **Why it matters:** It's the very first line read, and it's generic enough to belong to almost any consultancy. Nothing yet signals "we recognise your specific structural dysfunction."
- **Proposed Fix (illustrative direction):** "Your AI demo looked great. Then nothing changed. We fix the structural reason why." — final wording is yours, but the shift is from an abstract slogan to a one-line diagnosis this buyer recognises.

## 5. Team page is a weak trust signal
- **Gap:** CEO and CTO are listed with name and title only — no bios, no prior track record, no credentials, no LinkedIn.
- **Why it matters:** This buyer is being asked to trust an unfamiliar small firm to diagnose and fix something painful. With nothing to verify who's behind it, the only thing backing the pitch is the pitch itself.
- **Proposed Fix:** Add one or two sentences per person — relevant prior experience, what they did before Resilient ROI — and a LinkedIn link if willing.

## 6. Jargon load without plain-language gloss
- **Gap:** Nautical metaphor ("boarding to sailing the operating model") stacked on compressed method vocabulary ("KPI is the sum of capabilities and maturities of its people"), with no plain-language translation anywhere.
- **Why it matters:** The buyer needs to understand the value without already knowing DBJ CMM/BPT vocabulary. Several lines currently require that knowledge to parse at all.
- **Proposed Fix:** Pair each metaphor/jargon-heavy sentence with a plain clause, e.g. after "sailing the operating model" add "(the day-to-day way your business, product and tech teams actually work together)."

## 7. Minor entity-naming inconsistency (nit)
- **Gap:** Footer says "Resilient ROI Ltd, UK"; Terms/Privacy say "Resilient ROI Limited (company no. 17415109...)."
- **Why it matters:** Small, but a buyer doing due diligence (e.g. checking Companies House) benefits from exact, consistent legal naming.
- **Proposed Fix:** Use "Resilient ROI Limited" consistently in the footer, matching the legal pages.
