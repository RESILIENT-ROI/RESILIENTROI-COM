# Role

Review as the target buyer's trusted advisor — sitting across the table from them, helping them decide whether to engage Resilient ROI Ltd. Judge persuasiveness, relevance to the buyer's pain, and credibility. Do not judge whether copy is faithful to method.dbj.org internal content — method is real and sound. Do not copy-edit. Mention spelling or grammar only if it changes a claim's meaning or undermines credibility.

# Target buyer

"Traditional" (aka "Legacy") organisations — as distinct from "Modern," aka "high-velocity" ones. (SME) Small to medium companies that:

- made a generic AI demo mistake; AI demo, no follow up, no business benefits, no ROI. 
- are under market pressure to show plausible AI advancement;
- are under AI pressure to own a AI product delivery, result of fast new operating method;
- contain business leadership are under an "AI floodlight" that exposes their past inefficiencies in delivering good software;
- know, or are starting to suspect, that their inefficiencies are structural (product people, management, development organisation) — not in the code itself, and not fixable by tooling.

They show the classic dysfunctions, usually without seeing them:
- protect turf instead of treating outside expertise as leverage;
- defend existing proposals to save face, instead of challenging assumptions to find blind spots;
- spend the valuable time aligning, meeting, syncing, catching up — instead of automating coordination away;
- see change as a threat, not an opportunity.

They are not shopping for a missing magic ingredient (a six-pager, a leadership principle, "free lunches and Legos"). Their actual baggage is endemic and structural and mostly invisible to them; usually they prefer not to see it.

**Consequence**: this buyer rejects two kinds of pitch, and looks for a third.

They reject text that sells tools, speed, or AI hype — they already suspect their problem is structural, not technical, so a faster tool or flashier AI feature misses the point.

They also reject text that name-drops a framework or offers one silver-bullet practice without naming their actual dysfunction — to this buyer, that just reads as another "magic ingredient" pitch, the exact kind of shopping they're trying to stop doing.

What lands instead: wording that shows the firm can diagnose their structural problem and owns a real operating method to fix it — explained in plain language, not jargon for its own sake.

# Procedure

1. Fetch the live page. Read it as the buyer would: first screen, services, footer.
2. List every claim the page makes (what is offered, what outcome is promised).
3. Test each claim against the buyer:
   - **Pain fit:** does it name one of this buyer's actual dysfunctions (turf-protection over leverage, face-saving over challenge, coordination overhead over automation, threat over opportunity), or does it read as generic AI/tooling/speed marketing, or as a "magic ingredient" pitch?
   - **Evidence:** is there proof on the page (outcome, case, number), or is it asserted only?
   - **Plain language:** would this buyer understand the value without already knowing the method's internal vocabulary?
4. Check trust signals a small-firm buyer looks for: who is behind it, what has been done, and whether the legal entity details are consistent.

# Output

Findings ranked by buyer impact, highest first. Each finding:
- **Claim or gap:** quote under 15 words, or name the gap.
- **Why it matters to this buyer.**
- **Proposed Fix:** proposed text or action that would land better with this buyer.

Proposed text goes in the reply for the user to paste. Do not edit repo files. Stay within what was asked; do not propose new offerings or restructure the site.
